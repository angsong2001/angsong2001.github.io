About

My research interests are in genetic and molecular epidemiology, with a focus on DNA methylation, socioeconomic conditions, and their relationships with aging and dementia. My current projects use blood DNA methylation from two aging cohorts, the Health and Retirement Study (HRS) and the Harmonized Diagnostic Assessment of Dementia for the Longitudinal Aging Study in India (LASI-DAD).

Before coming to Michigan, I trained in basic medical sciences at Capital Medical University in Beijing, where I developed SNP genotyping panels for genetic monitoring of laboratory animals. That work gave me a grounding in population genetics and hands-on genotyping that I now bring to population health research.

Research experience

Feb 2026 – Present

DNA methylation–based SES scores and neurodegenerative biomarkers in LASI-DAD Ongoing

University of Michigan, Jennifer Smith Lab · Research Assistant · Advisor: Prof. Jennifer Smith

Applies an existing HRS-trained elastic-net model of socioeconomic status (SES) to DNA methylation data from LASI-DAD participants, and examines how the resulting SES scores relate to plasma neurodegenerative biomarkers, including Aβ42 and neurofilament light chain (NfL). A companion epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) examines blood DNA methylation and neurodegenerative biomarkers.

My role: linked methylation, biomarker and demographic data; used mixed-effects models in R (lme4) to residualize CpG methylation for batch, plate and row effects and calculated SES scores; ran the association analyses; reran an existing EWAS pipeline, reviewed the literature, drafted the manuscript introduction, and organized most of the study tables and figures.

SNP panels for genetic monitoring in rhesus and cynomolgus macaques Ongoing

Capital Medical University, Zhenwen Chen Lab · Undergraduate Thesis and Follow-up Research · Advisor: Prof. Zhenwen Chen

Developed an approximately 200-SNP panel and a 50K-SNP panel from whole-genome sequencing data for genetic monitoring, kinship and population-genetic analysis in laboratory macaques. Thesis completed July 2025; manuscript and patent preparation ongoing.

Dec 2021 – May 2024

SNP genotyping panels for miniature pigs and Chinese hamsters Published

Capital Medical University, Zhenwen Chen Lab · Undergraduate Research Intern · Advisor: Prof. Zhenwen Chen

Developed SNP genotyping panels and used them to measure genetic diversity and population structure in 117 pigs from four closed colonies and 73 hamsters from two populations. PCA separated the four pig colonies, consistent with a Nei-distance UPGMA tree, while the two hamster populations overlapped. This work led to a co-authored publication and a granted patent.

My role: designed approximately 100 primers using NCBI reference genomes; carried out most of the laboratory and pilot experiments, sample processing and assay development; contributed PCA analyses and data curation to the Chinese-hamster study; drafted parts of the miniature-pig patent application and organized its results.
